Quaternion log chart

10q and log10(q) for quaternions, and numerical checks that the log chart gives the Hopf structure of S1×S3. Everything runs in your browser.

Calculator

Enter a quaternion w + xi + yj + zk. The results use the principal branch.

Identities

Round trips, the branch limit, non-commutativity and the logarithms of −1 (from quaternion_log10.py).

Metric and hypercomplex structure

Pulls the flat metric back through the chart and compares it to the conformal cylinder form (from quaternion_geometry.py).

Locally conformally Kähler

Tests dΩ = θ∧Ω for I, J, K with θ = −2 ln10 ds, using finite-difference derivatives (from quaternion_lck.py).

Curvature

Ricci scalars computed from the metric alone via Christoffel symbols and nested finite differences (from quaternion_curvature.py). Accuracy is about 1e-5, not 1e-12.

Differences from the Python scripts: the Jacobian is analytic and higher derivatives are central finite differences, since JavaScript has no autograd. Checks run at random points inside the principal branch. They are numerical tests, not proofs, and the fact that S1×S3 has no global Kähler metric (b2 = 0) is taken from the literature.
